# Registered Reports

Registered Reports are a format for empirical articles in which the hypotheses, methods and planned analyses undergo peer review before the research is conducted.

The format is offered for hypothesis-driven quantitative research, data-based resources that include an exploratory component (evaluated on their ability to generate comprehensive data relating to a topic of broad interest) and methods comparisons across disciplines. We also welcome submissions proposing secondary analyses of existing data sets, provided that the authors have not had prior access to the data in question.

This format is designed to minimize publication and research bias in hypothesis-driven research, improve the design and robustness of resource papers, while also allowing the flexibility to conduct exploratory (unregistered) analyses and report serendipitous findings.

The process occurs in two stages.

**Stage 1:** Authors submit their study proposal. If editors judge it to be sufficiently rigorous and important, it is sent for in-depth peer review. Following review, the manuscript is either rejected, invited for revision or granted in-principle acceptance. In-principle acceptance confirms that the study will be publishable provided the authors pre-register the Stage 1 manuscript, follow the approved protocol, meet all quality checks and offer a sound interpretation of the results. Stage 1 protocols are not published in the journal; instead, authors register them in a recognized repository (publicly or under embargo). *Nature* also offers to host these protocols on a dedicated space on Figshare.

**Stage 2:** After completing the study exactly as approved, authors submit the final manuscript for re‑review and publicly share raw data, materials, code and lab logs (when relevant). Pending verification of adherence to the protocol, assessment of any exploratory analyses and a reasonable interpretation of the findings, the article is published regardless of outcome or statistical significance.

# **Guidelines for authors**

## Presubmission

**All Registered Reports must initially be submitted as a presubmission enquiry.** These presubmission enquiries should contain the research question(s), an explanation of why the project is suitable for the Registered Report format, and a general description of the intended data and proposed analyses. *Nature* will only consider Registered Reports that meet the editorial criteria of the journal related to the strength of evidence and potential impact of the work. We might also suggest that the study be submitted as a standard paper if we believe that the research would be better served by the standard peer-review process.

## Stage 1

## Submission

### Manuscript

Presubmission enquiries that are invited for submission will need to be written up according to the Stage 1 Registered Report template. This document will contain a description of the key research question(s) and background literature, hypotheses, experimental procedures, analysis pipeline, a sampling plan (statistical power analysis or Bayesian equivalent, when applicable) and pilot data (where applicable).

At the registration stage, reviewers will be asked to assess the criteria listed in the Guidelines for reviewers.

### Cover letter

The full Stage 1 Registered Report should include a cover letter with the following information:

- A statement confirming that all necessary support (e.g. funding, facilities) and approvals (e.g. ethics) are in place for the proposed research.
- An anticipated timeline for completing the study if the initial submission is accepted; this will be factored into the assessment of suitability of the Registered Report.
- A statement indicating whether the Registered Report is intended for research based on pre-existing data and if so, a statement confirming that the authors have neither looked at, downloaded, or analysed the data before receiving an in-principle acceptance of their Stage 1 report.
- A statement confirming that the authors agree to share their raw data, any digital study materials, computer code and laboratory log (if relevant) for all published results. In the case of sensitive data, we still require a statement confirming that there is sufficient information to ensure the work is robust and reproducible.
- A statement confirming that, following Stage 1 in-principle acceptance, the authors agree to register their approved protocol on a recognized repository, either publicly or under private embargo until submission of the Stage 2 manuscript.
- A statement confirming that if the authors later withdraw their paper, they agree to the journal publishing a short summary of the pre-registered study under a section entitled *Withdrawn Registrations* .

## Stage 2

Once the study is complete, authors prepare and resubmit their manuscript for full review, with the following additions.

If your preregistered plans need to change at any point (see Protocol deviations), please consult your handling editor for advice.

The editors will do their best to get the original referees from the Stage 1 report to re-review the Stage 2 resubmission, but we also reserve the right to add additional referees, should the original referees be unable to re-review or should additional, exploratory analyses require expertise not covered by the original referees. Reviewers are informed that editorial decisions for Stage 2 reports will not be based on the perceived importance, novelty or conclusiveness of the results (see Guideline for reviewers).

### Cover letter

The Stage 2 cover letter must confirm:

- That the manuscript complies with the requirements for public availability of data and custom code associated with the paper in a persistent repository where they can be freely and enduringly accessed. Within the manuscript, this information should appear in two separate sections, entitled ‘Data availability’ and ‘Code availability’. Read more information on Nature Portfolio’s reporting standards and guidance on preparing your Data and Code Availability statements.
- That the manuscript contains a link to the approved Stage 1 protocol in a standalone section entitled ‘Protocol Registration’.
- For Registered Reports based on primary data collection, no data for any pre-registered study (other than pilot data included at Stage 1) were collected prior to the date of in-principle acceptance. For Registered Reports based on reanalysing existing data, authors should confirm that no data (other than pilot data included at Stage 1) was subjected to the pre-registered analyses prior to in-principle acceptance.
- A summary of the additional exploratory analyses that were performed.

### Manuscript

Stage 2 submissions should include the following sections:

#### Introduction

- Apart from minor stylistic revisions, the Introduction cannot be altered from the approved Stage 1 submission, and the stated hypotheses cannot be amended or appended. At Stage 2, any description of the rationale or proposed methodology that was written in future tense within the Stage 1 manuscript should be changed to past tense. Any textual changes to the Introduction or Methods (e.g. correction of typographic errors) must be clearly marked in the Stage 2 submission. Any relevant literature that appeared following the date of in-principle acceptance should be covered in the Discussion.

#### Results

- The outcome of all registered analyses must be reported in the manuscript, Supplementary Information or Extended Data, except in rare instances in which a registered and approved analysis is subsequently shown to be logically flawed or unfounded. In such cases, the authors, reviewers and editor must agree that a collective error of judgement was made and that the analysis is inappropriate. In such cases the analysis would still be mentioned in the Methods but omitted with justification from the Results.
- It is reasonable that authors might wish to include additional analyses that were not included in the registered submission. For instance, a new analytic approach might become available between in-principle acceptance and Stage 2 review, or a particularly interesting and unexpected finding might emerge. Such analyses are admissible but must be clearly justified in the text, appropriately caveated, and reported in a separate section of the Results titled ‘Exploratory analyses**’. Authors should be careful not to base their conclusions entirely on the outcome of statistically significant post hoc analyses.**
- Authors reporting null hypothesis significance tests are required to report exact *p* values, effect sizes and confidence intervals for all inferential analyses.

#### Discussion

- Authors should discuss their findings, including any limitations of the study.

#### Methods

- The Methods should follow the main manuscript and should be the same as in the Stage 1 manuscript, except any descriptions written in future tense within the Stage 1 manuscript should be changed to past tense.
- Any changes or deviations from the original Stage 1 experimental plan should be clearly marked in the Stage 2 submission.

#### Supplementary Information, Data and Code

- We strongly encourage you to make the anonymized raw data, any digital experimental materials (e.g. stimuli etc.), and computer code (if relevant) freely available in a public repository. Authors are free to use any repository that renders data and materials freely and publicly accessible and provides a digital object identifier (DOI) to ensure that the data remain persistent, unique and citable.
- In cases where there are restrictions on data access, these must be explained and documented.
- Data files should be appropriately time stamped to show that data were collected after in-principle acceptance and not before. Other than pre-registered and approved pilot data and secondary data sets, no data acquired prior to the date of in-principle acceptance are admissible in the Stage 2 submission. Raw data must be accompanied by guidance notes, where required, to assist other scientists in replicating the analysis pipeline. Authors are also expected to upload any relevant analysis scripts and other experimental materials that would assist in replication.
- Supplementary figures, tables or other text (such as supplementary methods) should be included as Extended Data or standard Supplementary Information that accompanies the paper. Raw data and custom code associated with the paper can be provided through deposition in a persistent repository where they can be freely and enduringly accessed or as a supplementary data file when no appropriate repository is available. We strongly discourage providing large data sets in Supplementary Information; the preferred approach is to make data available in repositories. For more information, please read our full policy on data availability.
- A basic laboratory log might be asked to be provided outlining the range of dates during which data collection took place. This log should be uploaded to the same public archive as the data and materials.

#### Protocol Registration

The Stage 2 manuscript must also contain a link to the registered protocol (deposited following in-principle acceptance) in a standalone section entitled ‘Protocol Registration’.

### Manuscript Withdrawal and Withdrawn Registrations

It is possible that authors with in-principle acceptance might wish to withdraw their manuscript following or during data collection. Possible reasons could include major technical error, an inability to complete the study due to other unforeseen circumstances, or the desire to submit the results to a different journal. In all such cases, manuscripts can be withdrawn at the authors’ discretion. However, the journal will publicly record each case in a section called Withdrawn Registrations. This section will include the authors, proposed title, the referenced summary from the approved Stage 1 submission and brief reason(s) for the failure to complete the study. Partial withdrawals are not possible; i.e. authors cannot publish part of a registered study by selectively withdrawing one of the planned experiments. Such cases must lead to withdrawal of the entire paper. Studies that are not completed by the agreed Stage 2 submission deadline (which can be extended in negotiation with the editorial office) will be considered withdrawn and will be subject to a Withdrawn Registration.

### Incremental Registrations

There might be cases in which the information necessary to describe some additional study hypotheses can be obtained only after an initial round of data collection. In this case, an incremental registration will be necessary, meaning that the part of the Stage 1 submission that can be completed before data collection is reviewed, and once it has been deemed accurate, the initial data are collected and only the analyses necessary to generate the additional hypotheses/information required to complete the Stage 1 report are done. This new information is then added to the Stage 1 submission, which will be reviewed again. This option might be particularly appropriate when an initial experiment reveals a major serendipitous finding that warrants follow-up within the same paper. In cases when an incremented submission is rejected (at either Stage 1 or 2), authors will retain the option of publishing the most recently approved version of the manuscript. For further advice on specific scenarios for incremental registration, please contact your handling editor.

### Protocol deviations and changes at Stage 2

Authors are reminded that any deviation from the stated experimental procedures could lead to rejection of the manuscript at Stage 2. In cases where the pre-registered protocol is altered after in-principle acceptance due to unforeseen circumstances (e.g. change of equipment or unanticipated technical error), the authors must consult the editors immediately for advice. Ideally, this would be done prior to the completion of data collection.

Minor changes to the protocol may be permitted according to editorial discretion. In such cases, in-principle acceptance would be preserved and the deviation reported in the Stage 2 submission. If the authors wish to alter the experimental procedures more substantially following in-principle acceptance but still wish to publish their article as a Registered Report then the manuscript must be withdrawn and resubmitted as a new Stage 1 submission. Note that registered analyses must be undertaken, but additional exploratory analyses can also be included in a final manuscript (see below).

# **Guidelines for reviewers**

The review process for Registered Reports is divided into two stages. At Stage 1, reviewers evaluate the study proposal before data collection. Manuscripts can be rejected, invited for revision, or granted an in-principle acceptance, a commitment to publish the final article as long as the study is conducted following the Stage 1 protocol, meets all pre-specified quality checks, and presents a coherent, evidence-based interpretation of the results. The reviewers will assess this at Stage 2.

Stage 1 manuscripts contain only an Introduction, Methods (including proposed analyses), and Pilot Data (where applicable). Reviewers are asked to evaluate:

1. The importance of the research question(s) and impact for a broad, multidisciplinary audience
2. The value of the data to be generated (and the ability of these data to generate relevant findings for the field) for data-based resources that include an exploratory component
3. For methods comparisons, the importance of the comparative analysis
4. The extent to which the proposed study can satisfactorily answer the research question(s)
5. The logic, rationale and plausibility of the proposed hypotheses
6. The soundness and feasibility of the methodology and analysis pipeline (including statistical power analysis)
7. Whether the clarity and degree of methodological detail would be sufficient to replicate exactly the proposed experimental procedures and analysis pipeline
8. Whether the authors provide a sufficiently clear and detailed description of the methods to prevent undisclosed flexibility in the experimental procedures or analysis pipeline
9. Whether the authors have considered sufficient outcome-neutral conditions (e.g. absence of floor or ceiling effects; positive controls) for ensuring that the results obtained are able to test the stated hypotheses.
10. In case of more exploratory research, whether the analysis and sampling plan is rigorous enough.
11. In the case of data synthesis and meta-analyses, whether the authors have followed suitable reporting guidelines (e.g. PRISMA).

Following completion of the study, authors will complete the manuscript, including Results and Discussion sections. These Stage 2 manuscripts will more closely resemble a regular article format. The manuscript will then be returned to the reviewers, who will be asked to assess:

1. Whether the data are able to test the authors’ proposed hypotheses by satisfying the approved outcome-neutral conditions (such as quality checks, positive controls)
2. Whether the Introduction, rationale and stated hypotheses are the same as the approved Stage 1 submission (required)
3. Whether the authors adhered precisely to the registered experimental procedures
4. Whether any unregistered post hoc analyses added by the authors are justified, methodologically sound, and informative
5. Whether the authors’ conclusions are justified given the data
6. Whether the new exploratory analyses are sound and that the results are accurately reported and interpreted.

Reviewers at Stage 2 might suggest that authors report additional post hoc tests on their data; however, authors are not obliged to do so unless such tests are necessary to satisfy one or more of the Stage 2 review criteria. Please note that editorial decisions at Stage 2 are only based on the soundness of the paper and not on the perceived importance, novelty, or conclusiveness of the results.
